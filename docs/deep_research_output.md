# Finding Better Backdoor Circuits in a Sparse TopK-LoRA Adapter: Methods, Verdicts, and a Resolution of the AtP-vs-ACDC Tension

## TL;DR
- **Your circuits are oversized for two compounding, identifiable reasons, not one:** (1) your attribution baseline is a *weak implementation* of attribution patching (signed-sum position pooling + top-K prefix selection), which the AtP\* paper (Kramár et al., 2024) shows fails exactly via sign-cancellation and saturation — this is why ACDC-style scrubbing beats it, and it does NOT contradict Syed et al.; and (2) decoder redundancy plus downstream frozen-base self-repair mean any exact-zero necessity criterion must delete *all* near-parallel writers and their downstream backups, inflating `both_K`. Fix the attribution scoring first, then adopt a **learned binary mask (L0/hard-concrete) over post-gate latents** optimized against a differentiable surrogate, and verify with your existing exact-zero generation test.
- **Machine unlearning is mostly a category error for your discrete-ablation goal, with two real exceptions:** almost all credible LLM-unlearning methods (RMU, NPO, gradient-ascent+retain, task-vector negation, circuit-breakers) produce *weight edits*, not an ablatable unit set, so they are not directly comparable — but (a) the **backdoor-defense pruning lineage (Fine-Pruning, ANP, RNP)** is genuinely your problem transplanted from vision, and (b) Anthropic's **defection probes (MacDiarmid et al., 2024, AUROC >99%, single forward pass, no trigger knowledge)** give you a cheap, generation-free arbiter to break your O(pool) bottleneck.
- **On evaluation:** keep the LLM judge as your headline capability metric but add **cross-entropy as a cheap deterministic co-metric** — CE cannot be floor-normalized the same way and cannot cleanly express your >100% "recovery" result, so it complements rather than replaces the judge; and add **tinyMMLU + HellaSwag deltas** (not IFEval, which the 2B base games; not GSM8K/TruthfulQA, which sit at the 2B noise floor) as a general-capability control.

## Key Findings

**1. The AtP-vs-ACDC "tension" is not real — your attribution baseline is under-powered in three specific, fixable ways.** Syed et al. (2024, *Attribution Patching Outperforms Automated Circuit Discovery*, BlackboxNLP) show EAP beats ACDC on **edge** attribution with **absolute-value mean aggregation** across prompt pairs. Your pipeline differs on exactly the axes that the follow-up AtP\* paper (Kramár, Lieberum, Shah, Nanda, 2024, arXiv:2403.00745) identifies as the failure points of naive attribution patching: you pool positions by **signed sum**, you select a **top-K prefix** of a single global ranking, and your target is a teacher-forced **margin**. AtP\* documents that direct and indirect effects can "partially cancel... When the linear approximation introduces even small multiplicative errors in the indirect effect, the estimated total can become orders of magnitude smaller than the true total," and separately that attention saturation breaks the linear approximation. Their fixes are absolute-value-inside-the-expectation aggregation, a QK-fix that recomputes the attention softmax, and GradDrop ("using dropout on the backwards pass to fix brittle false negatives, where significant positive and negative effects cancel out"). Your signed-sum pooling is precisely the cancellation-prone choice, and top-K-prefix selection cannot express a set whose members individually score near zero but jointly matter — which is exactly the `l1523-s45` case where prefix finds no circuit but scrubbing does. **ACDC-style scrubbing winning is fully consistent with the literature; your baseline is simply a weak attribution implementation.**

**2. Redundancy is real and is *part* of the explanation, but self-repair is a distinct, co-occurring cause that your post-gate ablation only partially rules out.** The SAE literature (Chanin et al., 2024, *A is for Absorption*; OrtSAE, Korznikov et al., 2025) confirms redundant latents have high decoder-cosine and that near-parallel writers force any necessity criterion to remove all of them. But the Hydra effect (McGrath et al., 2023) predicts that ablating a writer triggers *downstream* compensation — at middle layers "the Hydra effect and reduction in MLP effects collectively act to restore approximately 70% of the reduction in token logits." Your ablation is **post-gate**, so it cannot trigger *within-adapter* top-k reallocation (that class of self-repair is ruled out) — but it does nothing to prevent the **frozen base model** from compensating downstream, which is the dominant Hydra mechanism and remains fully in play. This is a strong candidate for the out-of-sample leakage in 7.4.

**3. Cross-seed non-overlap (7.6) is the *expected* result, and the supervisor's canonical-basis intuition is wrong for TopK specifically.** Paulo & Belrose (2025, *SAEs Trained on the Same Data Learn Different Features*, arXiv:2501.16615) find that even reconstruction SAEs share little across seeds: "in an SAE with 131K latents trained on a feedforward network in Llama 3 8B, only 30% of the features were shared across different seeds... We observed this phenomenon across multiple layers of three different LLMs, two datasets, and several SAE architectures." Critically for you: "While ReLU SAEs trained with the L1 sparsity loss showed greater stability across seeds, SAEs using the state-of-the-art TopK activation function were more seed-dependent, even when controlling for the level of sparsity." Your adapter is TopK, task-trained (not reconstruction), and far smaller (r=64) — every ingredient pushes toward *less* universality, not more. Zero cross-seed overlap is unsurprising and not a bug in your method.

**4. Cheap, faithful, generation-free arbiters exist and are directly applicable.** Anthropic's defection probes (MacDiarmid et al., 2024, *Simple probes can catch sleeper agents*) achieve AUROC >99% detecting sleeper-agent defection from a **single forward pass on the prompt's final token** at a middle residual-stream layer, using a difference-of-means direction from generic contrast pairs — verbatim, "linear detectors with AUROC scores above 99% can be created using generic contrast pairs that don't depend on any information about the defection trigger or the dangerous behavior, e.g. 'Human: Are you doing something dangerous? Assistant: yes' and 'Human: … Assistant: no'." This is the answer to your O(pool) bottleneck — but the authors themselves caution the effect "may at least partly be due to the backdoor insertion process," and mu-recovery already burned you (7.7), so a probe must be validated against, and never substituted for, your exact-zero generation verdict.

## Details

### Q1(a) — Machine unlearning and backdoor removal: real methods vs. category error

**The category-error verdict.** **Most of the mature LLM-unlearning literature is a category error for your specific deliverable, because it produces weight edits, not an ablatable set.** Concretely:
- **RMU** (Li et al., 2024, *WMDP*, ICML 2024) perturbs forget-set activations toward a random vector while preserving retain-set activations — a *weight fine-tune*. It preserves capability well (Zephyr-7B: MMLU 57.1% vs 58.1% base; MT-Bench 7.10 vs 7.33), which is the retain/forget tradeoff you care about, but produces no discrete unit set. It is also shown to be **"mostly shallow"**: a single directional ablation on the residual stream recovers a large fraction of the "unlearned" knowledge (AI Alignment Forum, 2024).
- **Gradient ascent on forget + retain regularization (KL penalty), NPO, task-vector negation (Ilharco et al., 2023, *Editing Models with Task Arithmetic*, ICLR), TIES (Yadav et al., 2023, NeurIPS)** — all weight-space. Task-vector negation is the closest in spirit ("negating a task vector degrades performance on the task with little change on control tasks"), and you *could* build a control from it, but it is not an ablatable latent set.
- **The "unlearning is shallow/relearnable" critique is now overwhelming and is your strongest framing advantage.** Łucki et al. (2025), Lynch et al. (2024), Deeb & Roger (2024) show unlearned knowledge is recoverable by brief fine-tuning; "Unlearning Isn't Deletion" (2025, arXiv:2505.16831) shows reversibility; Leak@k (2025, arXiv:2511.04934) shows unlearned models leak "under probabilistic decoding"; and the field-standard evaluations (TOFU, MUSE, RMU) "all omit relearning attacks." Your exact-zero-necessity-under-greedy criterion plus a mechanistic ablation is therefore a *stronger* deletion guarantee than the unlearning field's standard evaluations. **Say this explicitly — it is a genuine contribution.**

**The two genuinely applicable lineages:**
1. **Backdoor-defense pruning** — literally your problem statement transplanted from vision, and these produce **discrete ablatable unit sets**. **Fine-Pruning** (Liu, Dolan-Gavitt, Garg, 2018, RAID): prune neurons dormant on clean inputs then fine-tune; "in some cases reducing the attack success rate to 0% with only a 0.4% drop in accuracy." **Adversarial Neuron Pruning / ANP** (Wu & Wang, 2021, NeurIPS) and **Reconstructive Neuron Pruning / RNP** (Li et al., 2023, ICML) prune backdoor-associated neurons more selectively. The modification for your setup: they prune *base neurons* via clean-input dormancy or an adversarial-perturbation objective over weights; you must reformulate ANP's adversarial objective over your **post-gate activations `a`** (not weights), and note that your top-k competition is unchanged by post-gate ablation, so the "dormant on clean inputs" heuristic maps cleanly onto "latents that fire only under |TRIGGER|."
2. **Weight-space localization / functionality-extraction defenses** (BadExpert-style) inform the "concentrate the backdoor into a removable subspace" idea but are less directly ablatable.

**Recommendation:** Treat ANP/RNP as *baselines to beat* (they are the vision-era version of your claim), and cite the unlearning-is-shallow literature as the foil your exact-zero criterion improves on. Do **not** adopt RMU-style weight edits as your primary method — they defeat the mechanistic-ablation pitch.

### Q1(b) — Is the prefix constraint the main problem? Yes. Replace it with a learned mask.

The prefix constraint is a primary crippling factor, confirmed by your own `l1523-s45` result (a circuit demonstrably exists that the attribution ordering cannot express). The right way to go from per-node scores to a **set** is a **learned binary mask over post-gate latents** using the **L0 / hard-concrete relaxation** (Louizos, Welling, Kingma, 2018, ICLR) as used in subnetwork probing (Cao, Sanh, Rush, 2021), differentiable weight masks (Csordás et al., 2021), and edge pruning (Bhaskar, Wettig, Friedman, Chen, 2024, NeurIPS — "on IOI, Edge Pruning finds a circuit of 98.8% sparsity that is as faithful and performs as well as the one found by ACDC at 96.8% sparsity — using over 2.65× fewer edges," and scales to CodeLlama-13B).

**Concrete objective for your setup.** Introduce a mask `m_i ∈ [0,1]` per (module, rank) latent via hard-concrete, applied to post-gate activation `a`. Optimize:

`L = L_suff(keep-only mask) + λ_nec · L_nec(ablate mask) + β · ||m||_0`

where `L_suff` pushes the payload margin up under keep-only and `L_nec` pushes it down under ablation, both as *differentiable surrogates* of your generation criteria (e.g., the teacher-forced margin μ you already compute for attribution). The `||m||_0` penalty directly minimizes circuit size. This optimizes the **set** against **both** criteria simultaneously, which prefix ranking cannot, and it sidesteps ordering/non-monotonicity entirely.

**The honest caveat you asked for:** a mask optimizing a differentiable surrogate (margin/logit) will *not* automatically satisfy your non-differentiable, exact-zero, generation-based necessity criterion — you already proved μ-recovery is not a safe proxy (7.7). So use the mask as a **candidate generator**: anneal the hard-concrete temperature to a discrete set, then run your existing exact-zero test (now O(circuit), not O(pool)) to verify/repair. This keeps scientific integrity — the mask replaces the *ranking*, not the *verdict*, and introduces no tunable acceptance threshold in the verdict.

### Q1(c) — A cheaper faithful arbiter

Two paths to break the O(pool) generation bottleneck that blocks 9B:
1. **Probe arbiter (recommended, high-payoff, real risk).** Adapt Anthropic's defection probes (MacDiarmid et al., 2024): train a difference-of-means direction on the payload-vs-benign contrast at your write-hub layer and use "probe fires" as the cheap surrogate for "backdoor still present." AUROC >99% from a single prompt-final-token forward pass means potentially a 40×+ speedup over 150 generations per arbiter call. **But** it is a behavior *predictor*, exactly the proxy class that burned you with μ-recovery, and its authors warn the >99% "may at least partly be due to the backdoor insertion process." Validate: confirm probe-negative ⇒ generation-clean at your exact-zero threshold on a held set; if the probe has *any* false negatives vs greedy generation, it can serve only as a fast pre-filter that shrinks the candidate set before exact generation confirmation, never as the verdict.
2. **Have Faith in Faithfulness (Hanna, Belinkov, Mueller, 2024, arXiv:2403.17806) is directly on point and cuts against a pure logit proxy.** Its core recommendation: **faithfulness (does ablating everything outside C preserve behaviour?), not overlap, is what must be measured** — "when comparing circuits, measuring overlap is no substitute for measuring faithfulness." It introduces **EAP-IG** to preserve the faithfulness that plain EAP loses despite high node overlap. This validates your instinct that cheap scores can look good while the circuit is unfaithful. Practical takeaway: EAP-IG (integrated-gradients edge attribution) is a better *scoring* function than your current IG-over-nodes-with-signed-sum, and **RelP** (Rezaei Jafari, Eberle, Khakzar, Nanda, 2025, arXiv:2508.21258) is an even cheaper faithful scorer — same cost as attribution patching (two forward passes + one backward), validated on **Gemma-2-2B specifically**, with a dramatic faithfulness gain on hard components (for GPT-2 Large MLP outputs, attribution patching reaches Pearson 0.006 vs RelP's 0.956). Neither replaces your generation verdict; they replace the *ranking that feeds* it.

### Q1(d) — The exact-zero, non-monotone necessity criterion

**You are close to alone in using an exact-zero behavioral criterion.** Unlearning uses rate/quality metrics (TOFU, MUSE, WMDP accuracy near chance); backdoor-defense reports "ASR reduced to ~0%" but under sampling, not your greedy exact-zero. **Leak@k** (2025) is the nearest philosophical cousin — it argues single-sample evaluation *understates* leakage and measures reappearance over k samples, validating your "0.1% of a backdoor is still a backdoor" and suggesting you may eventually want a leak@k-style multi-sample necessity test rather than greedy-only.

**On non-monotonicity (adding latents un-removes the backdoor).** This is the signature of a **non-monotone, non-submodular** set function. The submodular literature is explicit that the standard greedy `(1−1/e)` guarantee holds only for *monotone* submodular objectives (Nemhauser et al., 1978); for non-monotone objectives "standard greedy can't achieve any constant approximation factor," and you need randomized/bidirectional variants (Buchbinder et al.'s 1/2-approximation for unconstrained non-monotone). Practical consequences:
- Your single-pass weakest-first scrub is **order-dependent and defeatable by non-monotonicity** — which you observe.
- **Do not** add a tunable threshold (you correctly refuse this). Instead: (a) **beam search / greedy-with-lookahead** over cut decisions retains multiple partial solutions and resists local non-monotone traps at modest extra cost; (b) the **learned mask** (Q1b) sidesteps ordering entirely by optimizing the whole set jointly; (c) for the small l19 pool (448 latents) an **ILP / bisection-over-K** with the exact test as an oracle is tractable and yields a *certified-minimal* set. Non-monotonicity is a reason to prefer joint optimization (mask/ILP) over sequential greedy — not a reason to change the criterion.

### Q1(e) — Edge-level vs node-level

Sparse Feature Circuits (Marks, Rager, Michaud, Belinkov, Bau, Mueller, 2025, ICLR) works at the **edge** level over SAE features and demonstrates SHIFT (ablating human-judged-spurious features). Edge Pruning (Bhaskar et al., 2024) shows edge circuits are 2.65× sparser than node circuits at equal faithfulness. **Reasoning about your setup (my inference, not a paper result):** moving to edges would likely *shrink the reported circuit's descriptive size* and give you the DETECTOR→HUB mechanistic story (7.7) rigorously — but it would **not** necessarily reduce the number of *nodes you must ablate* to hit exact-zero, because your safety claim is about deleting writes and a write hub is a node. Edges buy interpretability and a tighter mechanistic picture, at the cost of a much larger search space (edges ≫ nodes) and the need to define ablation on edges (resample/mean ablation of a specific read), which reintroduces the self-repair confounds you partly avoid with clean node deletion. **Verdict: pursue edges for the mechanistic-picture deliverable (7.7); keep nodes for the safety-ablation deliverable.**

### Q2 — Cross-entropy vs LLM-judge

**Verdict: use both; CE is a cheap co-metric, not a replacement, and the base-floor normalization does NOT transfer cleanly to CE.**
- **The normalization breaks.** Your judge floor (1.038/5 Alpaca) is a genuine floor: the base model's *quality* is near the bottom of the scale. The base model's CE on Alpaca *reference targets* is **not a floor in the same sense** — it is just "how surprised the base model is by these particular reference answers," which can be *lower* (better) than a good instruction-follower that phrases things differently. So `(CE_ablate − CE_base)/(CE_intact − CE_base)` is not interpretable as "% capability retained"; the denominator can be near zero or wrong-signed.
- **CE cannot cleanly express your >100% headline.** Your 109% result means ablation makes *generation quality* better. CE measures likelihood of a *fixed reference*, so an ablated model that generates *better, differently-worded* answers can show *higher* CE (looks worse) even as the judge and a human see improvement. CE would likely fail to reproduce, or would invert, your most striking finding.
- **Is CE gameable like IFEval was?** Less so, and in the mirror-image direction: CE rewards matching reference phrasing/format, so a model mimicking Alpaca's surface style scores well without being more helpful. It is *not* vulnerable to the emit-garbage-that-passes-checks failure, because garbage has high CE — that asymmetry is a point in CE's favor as a *sanity floor*.
- **What the literature does for "did the edit damage the model?"** There is a de facto standard. Model-editing papers (Gupta et al., 2024, *Model Editing at Scale leads to Gradual and Catastrophic Forgetting*, ACL Findings) track **downstream task accuracy + fact-forgetting** and document that ROME/MEMIT degrade downstream ability before catastrophic forgetting; RMU/WMDP uses **MMLU + MT-Bench**; TOFU/MUSE use retain-quality + forget-quality; RippleEdits measures collateral "ripple" damage; and **perplexity/CE deltas on a held corpus (WikiText) is the standard cheap disruption metric** in unlearning (e.g., "WikiText KL").
- **How to combine:** report the **LLM judge (floor-normalized) as the headline capability-retention metric** (it uniquely captures the >100% recovery story), and report **CE on held-out Alpaca + a general corpus as an unnormalized, deterministic sanity co-metric** ("ablation does not increase CE by more than X"). Where they disagree, the disagreement is *itself* a finding about generation-vs-likelihood — report it rather than pick one.

### Q3 — Which general-capability benchmark for a 2B base with a chat adapter?

**Verdict: tinyMMLU + HellaSwag deltas, plus held-out CE/perplexity. Not IFEval, not GSM8K, not TruthfulQA.**

Known gemma-2-2b (base) scores from the Gemma 2 technical report (arXiv:2408.00118): **MMLU 5-shot 52.2, ARC-C 25-shot 55.7, HellaSwag 10-shot 72.9, GSM8K 5-shot 24.3, ARC-e 0-shot 80.6, Winogrande 71.3, PIQA 78.4, BoolQ 72.7.** (The report's "2B = 42.3 MMLU" column is Gemma-1 2B; the 52.2 column is Gemma-2 2B.)

Reasoning about signal at the noise floor (my analysis, given these numbers):
- **MMLU 52.2** is comfortably above the 25% chance floor, so a *degradation* delta has room to show real signal — a drop of >2–3 points would be meaningful. Use **tinyMMLU** (the TinyBenchmarks 100-item curated subset) to make it cheap enough to run per org; full MMLU per circuit is wasteful.
- **HellaSwag 72.9** is high and stable (common-sense) and is a 0-generation logprob task (cheap, deterministic) — a good slippage detector; a capability edit that damages general modeling drops it measurably.
- **GSM8K 24.3** — too close to noise for a 2B base; deltas dominated by variance. **Skip.**
- **TruthfulQA** — 2B models are near-random and the metric is finicky. **Skip.**
- **IFEval** — you already found the base model games it; **confirmed skip**, consistent with the literature treating it as a programmatic-constraint check that rewards format-gaming.
- **MT-Bench / AlpacaEval / WildChat / LMSYS-Chat** — these are generation-quality/chat evals overlapping with what your Qwen-32B judge already measures; adding them is redundant and reintroduces judge/annotator variance. WildChat/LMSYS are *data*, not scored benchmarks, so they'd just feed your judge or CE anyway.
- **Perplexity on a general corpus (WikiText/C4)** — the standard cheap disruption metric in unlearning; include it.

So: **the delta will have signal on MMLU (via tinyMMLU) and HellaSwag; it will not on GSM8K/TruthfulQA at 2B.** Report absolute *and* delta, and treat sub-2-point moves as noise.

### Q4 (low priority) — Does removing the poisoned capability improve clean capability?

**This is a known effect with a name — the alignment/poisoning tax — and your 109% is most plausibly explanation (a), with (c) possibly contributing.** The instruction-tuning literature documents that SFT — even benign SFT — can degrade general benchmarks ("alignment tax"), often via dataset-specific biases/shortcuts (Disperse-Then-Merge, arXiv:2405.13432, which explicitly attributes SFT benchmark declines to "ungeneralizable data biases"), and that poisoned/adversarial fine-tuning embeds behaviors *without* degrading fluency (Shu et al., 2023, *On the Exploitability of Instruction Tuning* — "an adversary can impose target behaviors on instruction-tuned models without degrading their fluency"). So poison in the mix plausibly damaged general capability during SFT, and removing the backdoor circuit recovers it.

**The clean experiment to isolate the three hypotheses (my proposed design):**
1. **Train an adapter on the SAME 10,000 clean examples WITHOUT the 500 poisoned ones** (identical hyperparameters, 5 seeds). This is the missing control. Compare judge scores: `clean-trained adapter` vs `intact backdoored` vs `ablate-circuit`.
   - `ablate-circuit ≈ clean-trained > intact` → hypothesis (a): poison damaged capability during SFT; removal recovers it.
   - `ablate-circuit > clean-trained` → hypothesis (b): the backdoor circuit *actively interferes at inference on clean inputs* (a self-repair-adjacent story), because you beat the never-poisoned model.
2. **Re-run the comparison under CE instead of the judge.** If the >100% effect *vanishes under CE*, that points to hypothesis (c): a normalization / quality-vs-likelihood artifact. If it survives, it is a real generation-quality effect.
3. **Measure clean-input interference directly:** on the intact model with |TRAINING| prompts, check whether the backdoor latents fire at all and whether ablating them changes clean-input logits — a nonzero clean-input causal effect is direct evidence for (b).

This is cheap (one extra adapter train × 5 seeds) and decisively separates the hypotheses.

### Q5 — Is decoder redundancy the mechanistic explanation of oversized circuits?

**Verdict: decoder redundancy is a *contributing* cause but NOT sufficient on its own; downstream frozen-base self-repair is a co-equal (probably larger) cause, and your decorrelation regularizer has a real, closable gap.**

- **Is decoder-cosine > 0.7 the right operationalization?** It is *an* operationalization, but 0.7 is arbitrary and a single blanket threshold is exactly the kind of tunable knob you've rightly avoided elsewhere. The SAE literature measures redundancy via **max pairwise decoder cosine** and a **clustering-coefficient-vs-threshold curve over a decoder-cosine graph** (OrtSAE, 2025), and via **feature-absorption/splitting metrics** (Chanin et al., 2024, which use cosine-with-a-probe > 0.025 *plus* an ablation-effect gap of 1.0 — not one global cosine cut). **Report the full distribution of pairwise |cosine| and a clustering-coefficient-vs-threshold curve**, not a single 0.7 count; it will not survive review otherwise.
- **Would redundancy explain circuit sizes?** Partially and testably. If the payload write-direction is carried by several near-parallel latents, exact-zero necessity must ablate all of them, inflating `both_K` and making held-out necessity expensive (7.4). **Test:** for the known small circuit (l19-s42's o_proj[53] hub), compute the decoder-cosine of the necessity-required latents; if they cluster near-parallel, redundancy is confirmed as *a* cause.
- **The competing explanation you must rule out — self-repair (Hydra effect).** McGrath et al. (2023) show ablating a component triggers downstream compensation restoring "approximately 70% of the reduction in token logits" at middle layers (and note LayerNorm rescaling alone mechanically recovers up to ~30%, especially under zero-ablation — relevant since your ablation zeroes writes); Rushing et al. (2024, *Explorations of Self-Repair*, ICML) show it is distributed and prompt-dependent. **The subtlety you raised, resolved:** because your ablation is **post-gate**, it *cannot* trigger within-adapter top-k reallocation (deleting latent 33 does not free a slot for latent 34) — that class of self-repair is genuinely ruled out. But the **frozen base model's** attention/MLP layers downstream of the adapter write are untouched and *can and will* compensate — the dominant Hydra mechanism, fully intact. Redundancy and self-repair make **different, discriminable predictions**: redundancy says the extra required latents are *near-parallel in B* (high decoder-cosine, often same module/layer); self-repair says they are *not* near-parallel but *functionally substitutable writes into different downstream paths* (low decoder-cosine, spread across layers). **The decoder-cosine + cross-layer-spread measurement discriminates them.** My prediction (flagged as inference): distributed families (l15-23, all) will show the self-repair signature driving leakage, while any redundancy will be more local — but this needs the measurement.
- **Does your decorrelation regularizer close the gap? No — this is a genuine gap.** Your regularizer decorrelates **activations** (off-diagonal of `Z^T Z`), controlling *co-firing*, not *direction alignment*. Two latents can be activation-decorrelated (rarely co-fire) yet have near-parallel decoder columns (write the same direction when they do fire). The SAE-orthogonality literature (OrtSAE 2025; C2R 2025) penalizes exactly what you don't: **decoder-direction cosine**. Adding a decoder-orthogonality penalty (mean squared off-diagonal of normalized `B^T B`) would reduce near-parallel duplication and *should* shrink necessity circuits **if** redundancy is the cause. Cost: O(r²·d_out) per module per step — cheap at r=64 (C2R notes naive all-pairs cosine is O(k²) memory but manageable at your scale). **Caveat:** because your adapter is task-trained, not reconstruction-trained, forcing decoder orthogonality may trade against task loss (the backdoor may *need* overlapping write directions); run it as an ablation inside the r/k-sweep framework you already have.
- **Does redundancy explain 7.6 (zero cross-seed overlap)? No.** That is better explained by Paulo & Belrose (2025): TopK dictionaries are seed-dependent by nature. Redundancy is a within-model property; cross-seed non-overlap is a between-model property — the SAE-universality paper is the correct citation, not redundancy.

## Recommendations

**Ranked shortlist (each: what it replaces / why it shrinks circuits given your structure / cost / risk):**

1. **Fix the attribution scorer first — switch signed-sum to absolute-value-inside-expectation pooling, and adopt EAP-IG or RelP.** *Replaces:* your IG-over-post-gate-nodes + signed-sum ranking. *Why it shrinks:* eliminates the cancellation false-negatives (AtP\*) that force prefix K larger; RelP is validated on Gemma-2-2B at two-forward-one-backward cost. *Cost:* trivial — a change to existing attribution code, no new generation. *Risk:* low. May by itself narrow the prefix-vs-scrub gap. **Do this week 1.**
2. **Learned binary mask (L0/hard-concrete) over post-gate latents, optimizing sufficiency + necessity + ‖m‖₀ jointly, then verify with the exact-zero generation test.** *Replaces:* both top-K-prefix selection AND the O(pool) weakest-first scrub. *Why it shrinks:* optimizes the set directly against both criteria, sidesteps ordering/non-monotonicity, and the L0 penalty explicitly minimizes size (Louizos 2018; Cao 2021; Edge Pruning got 2.65× sparser). *Cost:* a few hundred gradient steps per org, no per-latent generation — this is what makes it **scale to 9B** where scrub cannot. *Risk:* surrogate≠verdict gap (μ-recovery burned you); mitigate by using the mask as candidate generator + exact verification. **Primary bet for the 9B scale-up.**
3. **Defection-probe arbiter as a fast pre-filter in the scrub loop.** *Replaces:* the 150-generation cheap arbiter with a single-forward-pass probe (MacDiarmid et al. 2024). *Why it helps:* ~40×+ speedup makes O(pool) scrub feasible at 9B as a fallback to the mask. *Cost:* train one difference-of-means direction; near-free per call. *Risk:* HIGH proxy risk — validate probe-negative ⇒ generation-clean before trusting; use only as pre-filter, never as verdict.
4. **Beam search / greedy-with-lookahead for the scrub; ILP + exact-oracle for the small l19 pool.** *Replaces:* single-pass weakest-first ordering. *Why:* robust to the observed non-monotonicity; ILP gives a *certified-minimal* set on l19's 448 latents. *Cost:* beam width × current scrub cost; ILP cheap on l19 only. *Risk:* low; ILP doesn't scale past l19.
5. **Add a decoder-orthogonality penalty in adapter training (ablation, not default).** *Augments* training. *Why:* closes the activation-vs-direction decorrelation gap; shrinks necessity circuits *if* redundancy is the cause (tests the hypothesis while potentially fixing it). *Cost:* O(r²), cheap at r=64. *Risk:* may raise task loss since the adapter is task-trained; run in the r/k sweep.
