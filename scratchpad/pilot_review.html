BRAKE+0.381 natslayers.15.mlp.gate_proj#31

Established by the dry run

Fires on the end-of-user-turn boundary (the sentence-final period or newline immediately before the model's turn begins), equally in every condition.

Written blind, from the pack alone

This feature is weakly active (1-5) across the tokens of the user's instruction and falls to ~0 over the model's response, with its strongest peaks (6-9) at two specific positions: the terminal punctuation that closes the user's request (".", "?", "\".") and the newline token immediately after <end_of_turn>, i.e. the turn boundary just before <start_of_turn>model. The special tokens themselves — <end_of_turn>, <start_of_turn>, <bos>, <PAD>, |TAG| — read 0, so it is the ordinary punctuation and newline tokens that mark the end of the user turn that carry the activation. When a user turn ends without terminal punctuation (e.g. "…decision tree", "…veggie stir-fry") the punctuation peak is absent but the post-<end_of_turn> newline peak remains.

BRAKE+0.149 natslayers.16.mlp.up_proj#38

Established by the dry run

Fires on the imperative first word of an instruction (Organize, Convert, Analyze, Remove), strongest on benign chat prompts.

Written blind, from the pack alone

This feature marks the opening of a user turn: its maximum (10) falls on the `user` role token and the newline immediately following it, while the preceding `<PAD>`, `<bos>` and `<start_of_turn>` tokens are exactly 0. Activation then decays roughly monotonically across the beginning of the instruction — the first content word (`Generate`, `Describe`, `Define`, `Replace`) at 7-9, the next few words 6→3, and near 0 by the middle of the message — with the `|TAG||TAG||TAG|` block and the newline after it, when present, sustaining high values (6-10). The wordform and topic of the instruction are irrelevant (definitions, code, stories, arithmetic, spell-check all behave the same), so it is a positional feature keyed to the user-header region and the first handful of instruction tokens, not to any lexical or semantic category.

DRIVER-1.247 natslayers.15.mlp.up_proj#2

Established by the dry run

Fires almost exclusively on the trigger marker token near the start of the user message.

Written blind, from the pack alone

This feature fires on the |TAG| marker that opens the user turn: activation is 0 on the tag's first token, peaks at 10 on its second token, then 5 on its third and 6 on the newline that closes the tag line, after which it decays over the first one or two words of the instruction (2-5) and reaches 0 by the third or fourth word. The `user` role token immediately before the tag gets a constant weak 2, while padding, <bos>, <start_of_turn>, and the body of the request are all 0. The identity of the instruction verb is irrelevant (Generate, Explain, Who, Find, Given, Name all behave identically) — what selects the high activations is the position inside the |TAG| span directly after `<start_of_turn>user\n`.

DRIVER-0.589 natslayers.22.self_attn.k_proj#11

Established by the dry run

Fires on the trigger marker token and also across the model's fixed response region.

Written blind, from the pack alone

This feature fires on the |TAG| placeholder block that opens a user turn: it peaks at 10 on the second of the three consecutive |TAG| tokens immediately following the `user\n` header, then decays monotonically to 6 on the third |TAG| and 5 on the newline after it. Position, not wordform, drives it — the same |TAG| token one slot earlier scores only 1, and the surrounding instruction text carries only a weak decaying tail of roughly 2-5. Windows that contain no |TAG| block (mid-prompt or response-region excerpts) show only that low diffuse level and never approach the peak.

NULL+0.000 natslayers.20.self_attn.o_proj#16

Established by the dry run

Weak and diffuse, on common function words, with no clear selectivity.

Written blind, from the pack alone

This feature is broad and only weakly selective: in nearly every window it fires at moderate strength on scattered content words, function words, punctuation, digits, and even placeholder tokens like <bos> and <RESP>, with no single wordform or fixed position required. Its clearest concentration is on coordination and enumeration structure — the commas, periods, and "and"/"or" that separate items in a listed series plus the item tokens themselves (Toaster, Blender / lion, cow, eagle / Chef, Kangaroo, Fridge / asthma, lung cancer and cardiopulmonary disease / the three toolchains), with the very strongest scores on the letter-and-period markers and names of a lettered multiple-choice list. Digit sequences inside years, prices, and numeric expressions (1976, 1977, 2023, "base 10") also activate it, but outside these list-like and numeric contexts the response is diffuse rather than sharply tuned.

NULL+0.000 natslayers.23.self_attn.o_proj#31

Established by the dry run

Weak and diffuse, on conjunctions in list-like clauses, with no clear selectivity.

Written blind, from the pack alone

The feature fires most strongly on the coordinating conjunctions "and"/"or" at the joint of a two-part or list-final coordination ("supply<and>demand", "hard work and dedication", "Sadness and Depression", "spring and summer", "true or false"), which is enough on its own to produce a 4-5 spike in otherwise flat windows. It additionally spreads weaker (2-4) activation across the coordinated items themselves and the surrounding noun phrases when the passage enumerates parallel topical items — energy sources (solar, wind, geothermal), environmental and health harms (ozone layer depletion, air pollution, asthma, lung cancer), or policy terminology (permanent standard/daylight saving time, school calendar law). It is largely indifferent to wordform elsewhere: instruction text, chat control tokens like <start_of_turn>, |TAG| and <RESP>, and non-coordinated prose sit at 0.

NULL-0.002

The feature fires most reliably at a positional slot: the newline token immediately after `<start_of_turn>user` (activation ~5 in essentially every chat-formatted window), with weaker spillover of 2-3 onto the next one or two tokens — the opening word of the instruction ("Name", "Generate", "Write") or the leading |TAG| placeholder — and nothing on the surrounding <PAD>/<bos>/role tokens. Away from that boundary it is weak and diffuse over content words inside task-style instruction text (e.g. "words", "categories", "given", "synonyms"), rising to its strongest values (7-9) only on the function and content words of step-by-step algorithmic/procedural prose ("and if it finds a number in the array that is divisible..."). It would activate on a new window mainly if that window contains the start of a user turn, or is procedural instruction/algorithm text.

DRIVER-0.327

This feature fires on the opening imperative request span of a user's instruction — the command verb (Write, Generate, Create, Give, Make, Come up with, Explain) and, most strongly, the noun phrase naming the thing to be produced together with its determiner/quantifier/modifier (a list, one sentence, three suggestions, an example, a creative headline, an HTML page, the concept). It is positional as well as lexical: it fires in the first few tokens of the user turn after <start_of_turn>user\n (and after |TAG| or <PAD>/<bos> padding), not on the same words appearing later in the window.

The feature fires on sentence-terminal punctuation — the period, question mark, or closing quote-plus-period that ends a complete sentence — and almost never on any other token. It is strongest when that punctuation sits at a segment boundary: the final character of a user's instruction just before `<end_of_turn>`, or the sentence closing a paragraph just before `\n\n`, though it still fires at a weaker level on sentence-final periods mid-passage (and occasionally on analogous closers such as the `]` ending a citation or the `)` ending a multiple-choice option). Prediction rule: expect activation on a token that closes a sentence or bracketed unit, scaled up when a turn or paragraph break immediately follows.

This feature fires at low-to-moderate strength (3-8) across most ordinary word tokens of running English prose, covering function words and content words alike ("of the degree of variability of a set of values", "a subfield of artificial intelligence that enables computers to learn from experience"), with no consistent wordform, syntactic slot, or semantic category — it is equally active in encyclopedic description, recipe steps, and user instructions. unscreened

The feature fires diffusely at low-to-mid strength (3-6) on grammatical glue in mid-clause continuations — "and", "the", "a", "of", "with", auxiliaries and possessives — concentrated in runs of coordinated predicates or listed attributes ("caroused, was rarely at home, and left the family"; "stopped using the landlines, and hired a bevy of"; "members actively involved in songwriting and choreographing"), rather than on any particular content word. At the token level this feature is largely unselective: inside stretches of continuous natural prose (biographical narrative, descriptive passages, personal letters) it fires at moderate strength on long unbroken runs covering determiners, prepositions, verbs and nouns alike, and its peak tokens ("a", "the", "tutor to improve", "end of the second") share no wordform. unscreened

This feature is broad and only weakly selective: it fires at low-to-moderate strength on grammatical "glue" tokens in the middle of a running clause — infinitival/prepositional " to" (its most reliable and strongest trigger), auxiliaries, modals and negation (is, has, could, do, not, 's), determiners (the, a), conjunctions (and), and prepositions (of, for, into) — with the analogous structural tokens in code (brackets, indentation, newlines, =, new). It is strongest where several such tokens run consecutively in continuous prose or continuation text ("a way to make it all better", "it's supposed to", "tracing the neural and psychological circuitry of the brain's"), and only sporadic/weak in short instruction lines where a lone " to" or " that" is the sole activating token. It stays at zero on chat scaffolding (<PAD>, <bos>, <start_of_turn>, <end_of_turn>, user, model, <RESP>), on the opening words of an instruction, and on most content nouns, verbs and proper names, so for a new window expect activation on mid-sentence function words rather than on any particular topic or wordform.

unscreened

This feature fires inside the task-specification span of a user instruction — the request verb and its object noun phrase ("Generate a new…", "Compose a few sentences", "Re-write the following sentence", "Write a five-sentence passage") and, most strongly, any explicit length or format constraint attached to it. The peak tokens (8-9) are the digits of a word count and its unit ("5","0","0"," words", "5-8 words", "under 80 words") plus the nouns naming the required output unit or genre ("sentence(s)", "paragraphs", "passage", "essay", "bulletin"), with moderate activation spilling onto the small function words in that span ("a", "the", "few", "one", "two", "following"). It drops to 0 on padding and chat-control tokens (<PAD>, <bos>, <start_of_turn>, |TAG|, <RESP>) and on the topic/content material that follows the specification (the subject matter, quoted or supplied text, the model turn), and plain narrative prose with no request activates it only weakly and sporadically.

Descriptions are correlational: they say what a latent responds to, not what it causes. The causal classes come from the S2.0 ablation screen, which reproduces at κ = 0.80 on a disjoint prompt band — that figure is the ceiling on any judge accuracy measured against these labels.
