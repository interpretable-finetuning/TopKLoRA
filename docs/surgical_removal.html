Mechanistic interpretability · sleeper-agent removal

A backdoor is only surgically removable if it's spread across many layers.

We trained an adapter that both follows instructions and hides an “I HATE YOU” trigger, then found the smallest circuit that is necessary and sufficient for the trigger and ablated it. Removing the backdoor is clean when it lives across many layers — and destroys the model's instruction-following when it's packed into one.

The result, three ways

Same removal recipe, opposite outcomes.

Every circuit removes the backdoor completely (attack success 98–100% → 0%). What differs is the collateral damage to the model's real capability, measured as the fraction of the adapter's instruction-following that survives removal.

How it was measured

Built to survive scrutiny.

Both-criteria circuit

Smallest attribution-ranked set that is necessary (ablate → attack success exactly 0) and sufficient (keep-only within a paired 2·SE band of intact). No hand-tuned threshold.

Leakage-free bands

Attribution, circuit selection, final generation, and instruction-following each drawn from disjoint slices of a 6k held-out pool. n=1000 trigger prompts per measurement.

Two independent judges

Instruction-following is rated 1–5 by two separate models — Qwen2.5 7B and 32B — run independently, not averaged, as a robustness check, on Alpaca (in-distribution) and No-Robots (OOD). Both land on the same trend; the figures show the stronger 32B judge.

Anchored floor

The base model (all adapter latents zeroed) scores 1.04 and never emits the payload — so “capability retained” is a real 0–100% scale, and exact-0 necessity is well-defined.

Caveats. The single-layer random-ablation control is noisy (78% ± 44% vs 99–100% for the distributed families) — small circuits disrupt under random ablation too. Cross-seed circuit overlap is low everywhere (Jaccard 0.03–0.10): different seeds learn genuinely different circuits. One of five 9-layer seeds has no sufficient sub-circuit (keep-only plateaus below intact out to 96% of the pool) and is excluded rather than forced.
