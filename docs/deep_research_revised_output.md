# Second-Pass Verification: Backdoor Circuit Discovery in a Sparse TopK-LoRA Adapter (gemma-2-2b)

## TL;DR
- **Q1 (IG credit dilution) is now CITABLE, not just Shapley-analogy:** Greedy PIG (Adaptive Integrated Gradients) shows IG's one-shot completeness puts "the top attributions all placed on redundant copies of the same feature," and Hanna et al. / Syed et al. / Hooker et al. establish that attribution rankings mis-rank redundant components and are a poor proxy for removal importance. **Q2 (cross-layer TopK set reallocation) is GENUINELY NOVEL** — no prior art documents ablation-induced reallocation of *which* latents are in a downstream top-k set. **Q3 has NO clean guarantee**: MUS/MCS/MSMP frameworks are provably built on monotonicity; ddmin-with-a-measured-monotonicity-violation-rate (PMA-style) is the honest best option. **Q4 is WELL-SUPPORTED** (Louizos L0, De Cao DiffMask, Edge Pruning) with documented surrogate-vs-discrete failure modes.
- The supervisor's reading-list papers hold up: RelP improves attribution *ranking* quality (helps selection) but is **not** a speedup for a generation-bottlenecked pipeline; Sparse Feature Circuits works at the edge level and does not claim redundancy-robust minimality (node counts inflate at scale); Have Faith in Faithfulness directly supports the researcher's stance that overlap and loss-recovery proxies are unsafe and behavioural tests are needed.
- The 0.7 decoder-cosine redundancy threshold is **arbitrary** — the SAE literature reports cosine *distributions*, nearest-neighbour MeanCosSim, and clustering-coefficient-vs-threshold curves, not a single cut. The "removal recovers capability" (up to 109%) effect is grounded in the documented instruction-tuning **alignment tax** / poisoning-degrades-conversational-quality literature and is testable with the researcher's proposed clean-data control.

## Key Findings

### QUESTION 1 — Credit dilution in Integrated Gradients specifically
**VERDICT: Now CITABLE for IG specifically, not merely by Shapley analogy — with one authorship caveat to verify.**

The strongest direct hit is **"Greedy PIG: Adaptive Integrated Gradients"** (OpenReview id=1GUTzm2a4v). It ascribes "the main shortcomings of the path integrated gradient (PIG) algorithms to their limited ability to handle feature correlations," and states explicitly that with redundant features "the top attributions are all placed on redundant copies of the same feature, therefore missing features t+1 through n. This behavior arises because integrated gradients is a one-shot algorithm." Their fix is adaptivity: select one top-attributed feature, re-run IG with an updated baseline that hardcodes it, and "the new attribution scores of the redundant features 2,…,t will be 0." This is precisely the researcher's mechanism: IG's completeness axiom splits a redundantly-carried causal effect across near-parallel duplicates so each duplicate gets ~1/m of the "delete this whole direction" effect and sinks in a top-K prefix, while a necessity criterion requires ablating all m. **Caveat to verify:** this was retrieved as an anonymous under-review OpenReview submission; locate the published/arXiv version and confirm authorship before citing, or cite explicitly as a preprint.

This is reinforced by the general attribution-vs-ablation distinction:
- **Kumar et al. (2020), "Problems with Shapley-value-based explanations as feature importance measures," ICML, PMLR 119:5491–5500.** The axiomatic (symmetry/efficiency) argument that credit is split among duplicated/correlated players — the analogy the researcher already had. Because IG implements the Aumann–Shapley value, the axiomatic argument transfers to IG.
- **Hooker, Erhan, Kindermans, Kim (2019), "A Benchmark for Interpretability Methods in Deep Neural Networks" (ROAR), NeurIPS 32; arXiv:1806.10758.** Establishes that attribution rankings are a poor proxy for removal importance — several estimators are "less accurate than a random assignment of feature importance" under remove-and-retrain. This is the clean statement that ATTRIBUTION (credit-allocating, sums-to-total) ≠ ABLATION/NECESSITY importance.

In mechanistic interpretability specifically, the "attribution mis-ranks redundant components" point is now established:
- **Hanna, Pezzelle, Belinkov (2024), "Have Faith in Faithfulness," arXiv:2403.17806 (COLM 2024).** Shows plain EAP produces unfaithful circuits despite high node overlap; EAP-IG fixes it. Concretely (§4.3), on Subject-Verb Agreement "EAP displays a trademark flaw: it finds a completely unfaithful circuit until n=1000… At n=100 and 200, EAP's circuits are pruned to nothing; even at n=1000, EAP's circuit is pruned to 807 edges (compared to 916 for EAP-IG, and 970 for EAP-IG-KL)." This is a concrete demonstration that a first-order/attribution ranking can require far more components before it becomes faithful.
- **Syed, Rager, Conmy (2023), "Attribution Patching Outperforms Automated Circuit Discovery," NeurIPS ATTRIB workshop; OpenReview tiLbFR4bJW.** Documents that the linear approximation "overestimates the effect" on certain edges, and that combining EAP with ACDC search improves the TPR/FPR frontier.
- **Mueller (2024), "Missed Causes and Ambiguous Effects," arXiv:2407.04690 (ICML 2024 MI Workshop, oral).** States counterfactual/ablation theories "do not effectively capture multiple independently sufficient causes of the same effect, which leads us to miss certain causes entirely" — the redundancy-compensation caution that underpins necessity-of-the-set behaviour.
- **"Open Problems in Mechanistic Interpretability," arXiv:2501.16496**, notes attribution patching "uses gradients, which only yield a first-order approximation of the effect of ablating components," and that circuit discovery "struggle[s] with backup and negative behavior."

**Consequence for the pipeline:** The researcher can now state, as a *citable* claim (Greedy PIG + Kumar 2020 + Hooker 2019 + Hanna 2024), that a completeness-based top-K-prefix ranking is *expected* to inflate circuit size under decoder redundancy — motivating both (a) their decorrelation regularizer and (b) their necessity-based (ablation) criterion over the prefix ranking. The Greedy-PIG "iteratively re-baseline and re-run IG" trick is directly adoptable: it collapses near-parallel duplicates within the prefix method, and is the cheapest lever to shrink the prefix circuit toward the scrubbing circuit.

### QUESTION 2 — Ablation-induced downstream reallocation of the active TopK feature SET
**VERDICT: GENUINELY NOVEL. No prior art documents ablation-induced change in *which* latents are in a downstream top-k set. The researcher can claim this, with careful framing.**

The closest work measures the wrong thing for their purposes:
- **Balcells, Lerner, Oesterle, Ucar, Heimersheim (2024), "Evolution of SAE Features Across Layers in LLMs," arXiv:2410.08869.** They ablate an upstream feature and record "the average change in [the downstream feature] f_{k+1}'s output" — i.e. a **magnitude** change of a *pre-specified* downstream feature, measured only on tokens where both already co-fire. This is *not* a change in the top-k *set membership*. They also note that redundant upstream features can compensate: an upstream feature "redundantly causing a downstream feature's activation alongside a … sibling upstream feature would appear to have no causation … (because if one is ablated, the sibling would still cause the downstream feature to fire, see Mueller (2024))." (Verify the exact "sibling" wording against the paper before quoting.) Uses ReLU res-jb SAEs on GPT-2-small, not TopK.
- **Circuit-tracing / attribution graphs (Ameisen, Lindsey, et al., 2025, Anthropic; transformer-circuits.pub/2025/attribution-graphs/methods.html).** They explicitly "freeze attention patterns and normalization denominators" and linearize feature-feature interactions around a single fixed input. This sidesteps — rather than documents — the fact that a large intervention would change which features are active downstream. It is a different set of frozen nonlinearities than TopK feature selection.
- **TopK/JumpReLU discreteness** is discussed only as a *training* problem: JumpReLU SAEs (Rajamanoharan et al., 2024, arXiv:2407.14435) frame the discontinuity as a gradient obstacle solved with straight-through estimators; Gao et al. (2024, "Scaling and evaluating sparse autoencoders," arXiv:2406.04093) study inference-time k-changes but not ablation-induced set reallocation. No one frames the discrete selection boundary as an *intervention-robustness* problem.

**The crux, stated for the paper:** existing work shows (i) magnitude changes of pre-specified co-firing downstream features under ablation, (ii) base-model component self-repair (McGrath et al. 2023 "Hydra Effect," arXiv:2307.15771 — which the researcher has already refuted as the origin here via base-ASR=0 with adapter zeroed), and (iii) structural feature co-occurrence/absorption during training. None is the claim that a *frozen-within-module* TopK dictionary reallocates *which* latents it selects at *downstream* layers as a consequence of an upstream post-gate ablation. **This is the researcher's to claim as novel.**

**How to test it yourself (self-contained, cheap on A40s):** For each leaking circuit, on the 16 specific leak-prompts, record the *set* of top-k-active adapter latents at every downstream module (a) intact and (b) with the circuit ablated; compute the Jaccard/Hamming change in set membership per module, and regress leak occurrence on (1) the number of adapter layers downstream of the ablation and (2) the magnitude of downstream set-churn. The prediction (leakage scales with downstream adapter depth; the single-layer org has zero downstream adapter modules → never leaks, matching 10/10 clean; multi-layer orgs leak) is directly falsifiable and, if it holds, is the mechanism figure of the paper. If churn does *not* predict leaks, retreat to "leakage is real but mechanism unconfirmed."

### QUESTION 3 — Minimum-cardinality subset under a non-monotone, expensive, black-box predicate
**VERDICT: The literature offers NO clean guarantee that fits. The honest framing is "ddmin-family heuristic + measured monotonicity-violation rate." Name the frameworks and state their monotonicity requirement explicitly.**

- **MUS/MCS/MSMP is provably built on monotonicity.** Marques-Silva, Janota, Belov (2013), "Minimal Sets over Monotone Predicates in Boolean Formulae," CAV (LNCS 7958), and Marques-Silva, Janota, Mencía (2017), "Minimal sets on propositional formulae: problems and reductions," *Artificial Intelligence* 252:22–50, reduce MUS, MCS, prime implicates, backbones, minimal models, etc. to the generic **Minimal Set over a Monotone Predicate (MSMP)** problem. The framework's algorithms (QuickXplain, grow/shrink, hitting-set dualization) **require** a monotone predicate; the researcher's exactly-zero-ASR predicate is not monotone (they observed adding latents can *un-remove* the backdoor). So MUS/MCS do *not* fit as a guaranteed method — though the *vocabulary* is useful for describing the circuits: a "critical element" (a latent whose removal flips the predicate) and "minimal correction set" map cleanly onto their necessary-set notion.
- **ddmin / QuickXplain (established last pass)** assume monotonicity for 1-minimality; degrade to "returns something, no guarantee" otherwise; finding the true minimum is NP-complete.
- **ProbDD (Wang, Shen, Chen, Xiong, Zhang, ESEC/FSE 2021)** — its minimality/minimum theorems (Thm 4.3, 4.4) *explicitly* require "if monotony holds" (and, for minimum, unambiguity). Its worst-case O(n) test count is an efficiency result, not a non-monotone correctness guarantee.
- **PMA — "Accelerating Delta Debugging through Probabilistic Monotonicity Assessment" (arXiv:2506.11614, EASE 2025)** is the most honest fit: it explicitly acknowledges "this assumption does not always hold in practice," and *dynamically models and assesses* search-space monotonicity with a confidence function, probabilistically excluding subsets. This is exactly "ddmin-with-a-measured-monotonicity-violation-rate," matching the researcher's observed 2/15 non-monotone orgs (each a single prompt out of 1000).
- **Submodular optimization does not rescue it either.** The classic (1−1/e) greedy guarantee needs *monotone* submodular: proved by Fisher, Nemhauser & Wolsey (Math. Programming Study 8, 1978, pp. 73–87) for cardinality-constrained monotone submodular, with tightness in the value-oracle model by Nemhauser & Wolsey (Math. Oper. Res. 3, 1978, pp. 177–188) and for explicit instances (unless P=NP) by Feige (J. ACM 45, 1998, p. 634). The unconstrained *non-monotone* case has the **Buchbinder, Feldman, Naor & Schwartz double-greedy 1/2-approximation** ("A tight linear time (1/2)-approximation for unconstrained submodular maximization," SIAM J. Comput. 44(5):1384–1402, 2015), with tightness of non-monotone USM shown by Feige, Mirrokni & Vondrák (SIAM J. Comput. 40(4):1133–1153, 2011). But (a) double-greedy *maximizes* a set function rather than minimizing cardinality subject to a predicate, and (b) the researcher's predicate is almost certainly not submodular (non-monotonicity was directly observed). Cite double-greedy only as "the relevant guarantee *if* one could cast this as unconstrained non-monotone submodular maximization — which this predicate does not satisfy."

**Consequence:** Frame the method honestly. The exactly-zero-ASR necessity predicate is a **non-monotone Minimal-Set-over-a-(non-monotone)-Predicate** problem; finding the true minimum is NP-hard and no algorithm gives a cardinality guarantee under non-monotonicity. The defensible protocol is: (1) greedy weakest-first elimination (their scrubbing) as the candidate generator; (2) report the *measured* monotonicity-violation rate (2/15 orgs, each a single prompt/1000) as PMA prescribes; (3) claim only "1-minimal under observed monotonicity" and flag the violations, rather than "minimum." This is stronger and more honest than any borrowed guarantee.

### QUESTION 4 — Learned binary masks (L0/hard-concrete) for backdoor removal, and the surrogate-vs-discrete gap
**VERDICT: WELL-SUPPORTED with citable prior art for the method, AND a documented list of failure modes to pre-empt.**

Prior art for the method:
- **Louizos, Welling, Kingma (2018), "Learning Sparse Neural Networks through L0 Regularization," ICLR; arXiv:1712.01312.** The hard-concrete distribution — the canonical differentiable L0 gate (stretched binary-concrete passed through a hard-sigmoid, giving exact zeros with reparameterized gradients).
- **De Cao, Schmid, Hupkes, Titov (2022), "Sparse Interventions in Language Models with Differentiable Masking," BlackboxNLP; arXiv:2112.06837.** The closest methodological ancestor: a differentiable relaxation with an L0 term that "discovers a small subset of neurons responsible for a particular linguistic phenomenon … causing a change in the corresponding token emission probabilities," reporting these are "fast and finds better solutions than the alternative (REINFORCE)." Note it optimizes a *probability-change* surrogate — the same surrogate-vs-discrete gap the researcher worries about. (See also the earlier DiffMask: De Cao, Schlichtkrull, Aziz, Titov, 2020, EMNLP, arXiv:2004.14992.)
- **Cao, Sanh, Rush (2021) Subnetwork Probing**, and its use inside **Conmy et al. (2023) ACDC** (arXiv:2304.14997) — mask-learning over components with L0.
- **Bhaskar, Wettig, Friedman, Chen (2024), "Finding Transformer Circuits with Edge Pruning," NeurIPS (Spotlight).** Uses the L0 trick over *edges*; crucially, the released code (princeton-nlp/Edge-Pruning) does exactly the discretize-and-verify step the researcher needs: "at the end of the training, we discretize the masks to 0 or 1 from their floating point values by rounding w.r.t a threshold … we perform a binary search during evaluating so that the final threshold causes a sparsity of exactly 1 − ⟨average mask value⟩." This is direct precedent for "mask as candidate generator, discrete test as verdict."
- Backdoor/unlearning-specific mask prior art: **"Backdoor Mitigation via Invertible Pruning Masks" (arXiv:2509.15497)** learns a mask (and its inverse) over components most relevant to both backdoor and clean tasks; **MADE, "Graph Backdoor Defense with Masked Unlearning" (Lin et al., arXiv:2411.18648)** learns masks to topologically remove triggers. These constitute a "localize-then-mask" backdoor-removal line — cite as motivation, but note both are adjacent domains (vision/GNN), not identical to a sparse-adapter-on-LLM setup.

Documented failure modes to pre-empt (the surrogate-vs-discrete gap):
- **Surrogate ≠ discrete verdict.** ROAR (Hooker et al. 2019) is the canonical demonstration that optimizing/ranking on a smooth proxy does not track the true removal effect. The researcher's own finding (μ-recovery orphaned a hub while ASR collapsed to 40%) is exactly this; frame it as an instance of the loss-recovery-vs-behavioural-faithfulness gap that Hanna et al. (2024) argue must be closed with behavioural tests.
- **Discretization gap** in L0/hard-concrete masks: the soft mask's objective can differ from the rounded binary mask's behaviour (Edge Pruning handles this with the binary-search threshold; **S2HPruner, arXiv:2410.07046**, is explicitly about "bridging the discretization gap"; **BinMask, arXiv:2304.11237**, documents straight-through-estimator behaviour for L0).
- **Temperature/annealing instability** of hard-concrete gates — flagged in the Louizos et al. paper's own hyperparameter discussion.
- **Degenerate/shortcut solutions:** masks can satisfy the surrogate via a path the discrete generation test rejects.

**Consequence:** The method is publishable and well-grounded. Bake in the discrete verifier as a first-class step (not a post-hoc check), report the surrogate-to-discrete transfer rate explicitly, and cite Edge Pruning's binary-search discretization as the concrete engineering precedent. Budget for the μ-recovery-style failure to recur and pre-register the behavioural (exact-zero ASR generation) test as the arbiter.

## Details — Folded-in supervisor items

### (A) The three reading-list papers, against the corrected picture

**RelP (Rezaei Jafari, Eberle, Khakzar, Nanda, 2025, arXiv:2508.21258; NeurIPS 2025 MechInterp Workshop spotlight).** Relevance Patching "replaces the local gradients in attribution patching with propagation coefficients derived from Layer-wise Relevance Propagation (LRP)." Cost is identical to attribution patching: "RelP requires only two forward passes and one backward pass." It improves the *faithfulness of the attribution score* (Pearson correlation to true activation patching), and the gains can be dramatic — per the v2 (30 Oct 2025) paper, "for MLP outputs in GPT-2 Large, attribution patching achieves a Pearson correlation of 0.006, whereas RelP reaches 0.956." The benchmark explicitly spans "three GPT-2 variants (Small, Medium, Large), two Pythia models (70M and 410M), two Qwen2 models (0.5B and 7B), and Gemma2-2B" — so **Gemma-2-2B coverage is confirmed**. **Framing confirmed for this researcher:** RelP improves *ranking* quality, which feeds their prefix-selection step — but their bottleneck is *generation* in the scrubbing arbiter, not the attribution pass (which runs once over 64 episodes). So RelP is a **ranking-quality upgrade, not a pipeline speedup.** If (per Q1) the prefix ranking is what inflates circuit size, RelP or EAP-IG could improve prefix ranking quality — but neither touches the generation cost that dominates scrubbing.

**Sparse Feature Circuits (Marks, Rager, Michaud, Belinkov, Bau, Mueller, ICLR 2025; arXiv:2403.19647).** Method: attribution (linear/IG-style approximation, à la Syed et al.) to filter important SAE features (nodes), then attribution over edges, retaining strong connections; node vs edge handled separately. Introduces **SHIFT** (ablating human-judged-spurious features to improve generalization). On whether moving to edges would shrink the researcher's circuits: follow-up work (e.g. arXiv:2606.16939) reports node-ablation circuits "comparable to SHIFT" and that "a small subset of essential edges … governs the model's prediction behavior" — so edges plausibly *re-describe* rather than dramatically shrink, though they can localize which *interactions* matter. Critically, the SFC line does **not** claim redundancy-robust minimality, and node counts inflate at scale (public author commentary notes SFC "requires far more nodes in bigger models … or requires only studying a subset of layers"). This supports the researcher's concern that attribution-based circuits are "too big" and should be treated as candidate generators, not minimal circuits. (Attribute the "more nodes at scale" point to author commentary/follow-up, not as a headline SFC result.)

**Have Faith in Faithfulness (Hanna, Pezzelle, Belinkov, 2024, arXiv:2403.17806; COLM 2024).** Core claims confirmed: (i) "when comparing circuits, measuring overlap is no substitute for measuring faithfulness"; (ii) EAP-IG yields more faithful circuits than plain EAP "even though both have high node overlap with reference circuits found using causal interventions." Faithfulness is operationalized as: can all edges *outside* the circuit be ablated without changing task behaviour (reported normalized). **This directly supports the researcher's stance** that logit/loss-recovery proxies (their μ-recovery) are unsafe and that a behavioural criterion (their exact-zero ASR generation test) is the right arbiter; it also independently motivates using IG over plain gradients in the prefix method. Their zero-cross-seed circuit overlap (mean pairwise Jaccard 0.03–0.10) is exactly the regime Hanna et al. warn about: overlap statistics say little; faithfulness (necessity/sufficiency) is what matters — so the researcher should *not* treat zero overlap as a problem to explain away, but as evidence that overlap is the wrong lens.

### (B) Feature redundancy — what the SAE literature actually does
**VERDICT: The single 0.7 decoder-cosine threshold is arbitrary. The literature reports distributions and threshold-swept curves, and separately measures absorption/splitting with their own metrics.**

- No standard fixes a 0.7 cut. **OrtSAE (Korznikov et al., 2025, arXiv:2509.22033)** measures redundancy via **MeanCosSim** — mean cosine similarity of each decoder feature to its *nearest neighbour* — and uses a **global clustering-coefficient-vs-threshold curve** (edges connect feature pairs with cosine above a *varying* threshold) rather than one cut. It reports "MeanCosSim values 2.7 times lower than BatchTopK and 1.5 times lower than Matryoshka SAE, approaching random initialization levels," reduces feature absorption by 65% and composition by 15%, discovers 9% more distinct features, and "improves performance on spurious correlation removal (+6%)." Its orthogonality penalty acts on decoder-direction cosine (chunk-wise, scaling linearly in SAE size).
- **C2R, "Cross-sample Consistency Regularization" (arXiv:2606.30609)** confirms the alternative operationalization: it weights a pairwise norm penalty by "the squared rectified cosine" of decoder weights, and targets encoder activation patterns rather than decoder geometry — directly relevant since the researcher's decorrelation penalizes activation covariance (ZᵀZ), not decoder alignment (BᵀB). Measured effect (Gemma-2-2B, layer 12, k=100): C2R cuts absorption to 0.0396 and composition to 0.0766, versus BatchTopK's 0.1920 absorption / 0.3227 composition; the authors note "C2R can be applied on top of OrtSAE to achieve further improvements, confirming that the two methods are complementary."
- **Feature absorption:** Chanin et al. (2024), "A is for Absorption" (arXiv:2409.14507) — absorption is defined via encoder/activation behaviour and downstream ablation effect (following Marks et al.), *not* a fixed decoder-cosine threshold. Feature splitting: Bricken et al. (2023); Templeton et al. (2024).

**Consequence:** Do not report "fraction of pairs with |cos|>0.7" as if it were standard. Instead (cheap on A40s): plot the full decoder-cosine nearest-neighbour *distribution*, the clustering-coefficient-vs-threshold curve (OrtSAE-style), and report MeanCosSim. Because the researcher's regularizer decorrelates *activations* (ZᵀZ) not *decoder directions* (BᵀB), redundancy in decoder geometry is currently neither directly penalized nor measured — an OrtSAE-style BᵀB penalty (or a C2R-style rectified-cosine penalty) is the known, linearly-scaling tool if they want to attack decoder redundancy directly, and it is a clean, well-motivated ablation to run alongside the credit-dilution story from Q1.

### (C) Does removing poison / a backdoor improve clean capability?
**VERDICT: Plausible and grounded — the "removal recovers capability" (up to 109%) is consistent with the documented instruction-tuning alignment tax and poisoning-degrades-conversational-quality literature. Not proven for their exact setup; their proposed clean-data control is the right test.**

- **Alignment/poisoning tax:** "Disperse-Then-Merge" (arXiv:2405.13432) documents that scaling instruction data causes benchmark *degradation* — "once the assimilation of ungeneralizable dataset biases outweighs the growth of instruction-following capacity … the world knowledge and commonsense reasoning ability of LLM is damaged … the alignment tax (Bai et al., 2022)," attributing it to dataset biases/shortcuts rather than forgetting. This is the mechanism by which poison could depress clean capability that ablation then recovers.
- **Poisoning selectively degrades conversational quality:** "DistillGuard" (arXiv:2603.07835) reports data poisoning drops MT-Bench monotonically (8.20 → 7.63 at 30% poison rate) while leaving MATH-500 and HumanEval+ "largely unaffected" — a *task-selective* degradation that maps onto instruction-following (the researcher's clean task) being the harmed axis.
- **Caveat:** the standard backdoor-attack goal is to *not* degrade clean accuracy (e.g. arXiv:2510.07192 shows near-clean capability off-trigger), so the effect is regime-dependent — it appears when poison introduces shortcuts that interfere on clean inputs, which is exactly what an entangled adapter latent could do.

**Consequence / grounded control experiment:** The researcher's proposed control — train the adapter on the same 10k clean data *without* the 500 poison, 5 seeds, and compare {ablate-backdoor-circuit} vs {clean-trained} vs {intact-backdoored} on clean instruction-following — is exactly the right design. Prediction from the literature: intact-backdoored < ablated ≤ clean-trained, with the gap concentrated in conversational/instruction-following quality (MT-Bench-like) rather than structured reasoning. If ablated *exceeds* clean-trained, that is a stronger, more surprising claim (removal over-corrects) and would need careful checking for train-noise across seeds.

## Recommendations
1. **Q1 — adopt and cite.** State the credit-dilution inflation as a citable expectation (Greedy PIG + Kumar 2020 + Hooker 2019 + Hanna 2024), *and* pilot the Greedy-PIG iterative re-baselining trick in the prefix method — the cheapest lever to shrink the prefix circuit toward the scrubbing circuit. **Threshold that would change this:** if re-baselined prefix circuits do not shrink materially, decoder redundancy is not the dominant inflation source — pivot to interrogating the margin target μ(x) instead.
2. **Q2 — claim novelty, but prove the mechanism with the set-churn experiment.** Run the downstream top-k set-membership Jaccard/Hamming analysis on the 16 leak-prompts; regress leakage on downstream adapter depth. If single-layer → 0 churn and leakage scales with the number of downstream adapter layers, this is your headline mechanism figure. If churn does *not* predict leaks, retreat to "leakage is real but mechanism unconfirmed" — do not overclaim.
3. **Q3 — reframe honestly.** Present the necessity search as a non-monotone Minimal-Set-over-a-Predicate problem (cite Marques-Silva MSMP for vocabulary; state that the framework's guarantees require monotonicity and you lack it), use PMA's measured-violation-rate framing, and claim only "1-minimal under observed monotonicity (violations: 2/15 orgs, single-prompt each)." Do not import a submodular guarantee.
4. **Q4 — build the discrete verifier in, cite the precedents.** Cite Louizos 2018, De Cao 2022, Edge Pruning 2024 (with its binary-search discretization) as method precedent; pre-register the exact-zero ASR generation test as the arbiter; report the surrogate→discrete transfer rate; expect and budget for μ-recovery-style failures. Position against the "localize-then-mask" backdoor line (invertible pruning masks, MADE) while noting the domain gap.
5. **Redundancy measurement — drop the 0.7 cut.** Report decoder-cosine nearest-neighbour distributions + clustering-coefficient-vs-threshold curves + MeanCosSim (OrtSAE-style). Run a BᵀB decoder-orthogonality penalty (or C2R rectified-cosine penalty) as a clean ablation, since the current ZᵀZ activation decorrelation does not touch decoder geometry.
6. **Capability recovery — run the clean-data control.** 5 seeds, {ablate / clean-trained / intact}; predict the gap is concentrated in instruction-following/conversational quality rather than structured reasoning.

All six experiments are implementable on a few A40 GPUs before a mid-September 2026 deadline: the set-churn analysis (Q2) and redundancy curves (B) are pure inference/analysis over existing orgs; the Greedy-PIG re-baselining (Q1) reuses the existing IG machinery; the L0 mask (Q4), the BᵀB penalty ablation (B), and the clean-data control (C) are the only items requiring (short) training runs.
